A celebrity influencer or 300 micro-creators

A short comparison. Numbers link to their sources, read November 23-24, 2026.
  1. In the one celebrity we counted post by post, sponsored Instagram posts got about half the likes of her other posts (median 61K against 120K).17
  2. A celebrity's fee isn't public. 300 creator videos cost about $2,400 to $46,200 in creator pay, depending on who you hire, before product and ad spend.4,10
  3. Celebrity contracts can block a category, and the terms aren't public, so exclusivity is worth asking about early.

There are two ways to use micro-creators. They can post to their own followers, the way Graza launched, or you can pay for videos and run the best ones as ads under each creator's name. Most of this page is about the second, because it lets you choose the winners and who sees them. The 300 comes from Graza, which sent its oil to about 300 influencers at launch.

One example: what a celebrity's sponsored posts get

As one example, we counted every Instagram post from Jessica Alba, an actress and founder who does several brand deals a year and labels many of them, which makes them countable, from September 2, 2025 to November 15, 2026.

The sponsored median of 61K likes is about 0.3% of her 21.9M followers.17 Reel plays tell the same story: five of her six sponsored reels show play counts, with a median of 1.35M, against 2.46M for her 32 other reels.17 Reach, clicks and sales stay private to the brand, so even the results of her LUNA Bar campaign aren't public.3

What each one costs

A celebrity influencer

Celebrity fees aren't public and come through an agent. Even for the example above, speaker bureaus list only a speaking fee, as "contact us,"1 and the one public figure, $100K to $250K, is Motion's estimate of what brands spent running ads with her, not what they paid her.2 Reach is huge, but it's one voice and one look, and existing deals can block your category.

300 micro-creators

You buy the reach. The best videos run as ads under each creator's name, to whoever you target, so follower counts matter less. Someone has to brief, approve and sort 300 videos. Most will be average, and the point is finding the few that sell.

Graza, the closest comparison

Graza launched in January 2022 by sending its oil to influencers: more than 100 packages before its website went live, by one 2022 account,5 and 300 in all by the founder's count, "our entire advertising budget for our first eight months." The oil sold out in a day, and Whole Foods called the next day. By the company's own figures, sales passed $4M in its first year and $19M by the end of 2023, with $48M projected for 2024.20 Creators weren't the only cause: it also had a new squeeze bottle and Whole Foods early. Superfiliate, a vendor, says more than 1,000 creators have enrolled in Graza's program (undated),11 and about 320 ads from Graza's own page were running on Meta on November 23, 2026.21

What to copy from Graza, and what to avoid

Small creators first, big ones at launch. Micro-creators (25K to 150K followers) got the oil weeks early. Big ones got it right before launch, because "if they posted pre-launch, there would be nothing for their followers to buy." A social media consultant on the launch credited the smaller creators with helping it sell out.5

Keep the regulars. In the past year 1.6K creators posted for Graza (Modash counts creators who posted), but only 37 (2%) posted in three or more months.22 The program lives on the few who keep posting, so find them and keep them.

Demand ahead of operations. After year one's damaged bottles and peeling labels, the founder emailed about 35,000 customers to apologize.20

What's different since 2022. Graza launched on free product and organic posts. Creators today often expect pay or a commission, much of the reach comes from running their videos as ads under their names, and creators can sell inside TikTok Shop and, since April 2026, inside Instagram Reels.24

How running 300 creators works

It's a loop, not a one-off. The first round finds what sells, and every round after is briefed on the winners.

  1. Write one brief. What to film, the hook in the first two seconds, the claim to make, what not to say.
  2. Pick creators on a marketplace and ship each a box of product.
  3. Approve, ask for a revision, or reject each video before anyone is paid.
  4. Run the best ones as Meta partnership ads from your ad account, shown under the creator's name.
  5. Cut the losers weekly, spend more on the winners, and brief the next round on what worked.Then back to step 1

Why step 4 matters: one brand owner posted on X that the same video returned $6.37 in sales per $1 of ads ($17 per sale) under the creator's name, against $1.41 per $1 ($102 per sale) from the brand's own page.13 That's one brand's test, not a benchmark, but it's the reason the ad route is worth testing.

What Kosterina already has

Kosterina already makes founder videos on how to tell real olive oil from fake,25 and the best-performing ones could double as a brief for creators. It already pays in-store brand ambassadors, mainly at Whole Foods,12 and creators near those same stores would be the online version of that team. Creators already post about it, from a campaign on Hummingbirds, a micro-creator platform, sending shoppers to Walmart in November18 to Dr. Nick Norwitz (145K followers) sharing a code for the olive oil shots.19 Other olive oil brands are active too: a food creator posted a paid Graza partnership in November,15 and a creator posted about a lunch with Cobram Estate in October.16

Measuring the shelf. Codes and links miss in-store buyers. Run creator ads around one group of stores, hold a similar group back, and compare the sales.

When each one makes sense

Creators fit when the goal is learning which message sells olive oil to which buyer and owning a library of ads. For $2,400 to $46,200 in creator pay, plus product and a test ad budget, each round tells you what worked. A celebrity post can't tell you that.

A celebrity fits when the goal is national awareness for the shelf. There's survey support for it: in a global DoubleVerify survey of 22,000 consumers (surveyed February, published October 2026), 25% said mega-influencers with 1M+ followers had the most impact on their purchases, against 18% for micro.7 (DoubleVerify is an ad-verification vendor, and this measures what people say, not sales.) A celebrity makes sense when all four of these hold:

  • The goal is credibility with retail buyers or national awareness, not online sales you can track.
  • The contract lets you run their content as paid ads, for long enough to matter.
  • Their other deals don't block your category.
  • There's budget for paid media behind them. One organic post isn't a campaign.

Judge every option on the same five numbers:

  1. Cost per 1,000 people reached
  2. Clicks and code sales
  3. Cost per new customer
  4. Sales in stores near the campaign against similar stores without it
  5. How long you can rerun the content as ads

The store number is the hard one.

The two aren't exclusive. Creators can find the message first, and a celebrity can say it later.

Two more examples: Olipop and Shapermint

Olipop (soda). About 1,900 creators, paid in $36 sample packs plus a commission on sales. Creators drove 12% of total sales in 2024, up from 4% in 2021, and micro-creators' share of those sales rose from 3% to about 20%.6 (These are the vendor's figures.)

Shapermint (shapewear, TikTok Shop). It sells to women 35 to 65 and works with more than 5,000 creators on TikTok Shop, 60% of its top ones over 35. It reports $1.5M in TikTok Shop sales in the first four months of 2026 and projects about $10M for the year (its own figures).26 Worth copying: creators who look like the buyer, filmed at home; a weekly brief built from the winners; a move from agencies to its own creator team;26 and a pay ladder of 10% commission plus free product, rising to 20% after 15 sales and 3 posts in 30 days.27 Its winning hook shows the garment doesn't slip or tug, a worry food doesn't have, and oil is a repeat buy, so creators are best judged on reorders, not just the first sale.
