What each one costs

Jessica Alba

Her speaker bureaus list her fee as "contact us,"1 so the price comes from her agent. About 22M Instagram and 10M TikTok followers.2 She's LUNA Bar's ambassador,3 and she still owns 5.4% of The Honest Company, the wellness brand she founded.4

300 micro-creators

You buy the reach. The best videos run as ads under each creator's name, to whoever you target, so follower counts matter less. Someone has to brief, approve and sort 300 videos. Most will be average, and the point is finding the few that sell.

Graza, the closest comparison

Graza launched in January 2022 by sending 300 packages to food creators, which the founder calls "our entire advertising budget for our first eight months." The oil sold out in a day, and Whole Foods called the next day.23 Today it runs more than 1,000 creators on commission (the vendor's figure)13 and about 440 active Meta ads.24

What to copy from Graza, and what to avoid

Small creators first, big ones at launch. Micro-creators (25K to 150K followers) got the oil weeks early. Big ones got it right before launch, because "if they posted pre-launch, there would be nothing for their followers to buy." Its creator lead says the smaller creators "helped sell out the launch."6

Keep the regulars. In the past year 1.6K creators posted for Graza, but only 37 (2%) posted in three or more months.25 The program lives on the few who keep posting, so find them and keep them.

Demand ahead of operations. After year one's damaged bottles and peeling labels, the founder emailed about 35,000 customers to apologize.23 300 creators can create a spike the warehouse has to meet.

Crediting creators for everything. Graza also had a new squeeze bottle and Whole Foods early. No public figure shows how much of its sales came from creators.

What's different since 2022. Graza launched on free product and organic posts. Today creators mostly expect pay or a commission, the reach comes from running their videos as ads under their names, and creators can sell inside TikTok Shop and, since April 2026, inside Instagram Reels.27 Graza itself now pays commission and runs ads.

How running 300 creators works

It's a loop, not a one-off. The first round finds what sells, and every round after is briefed on the winners.

  1. Write one brief. What to film, the hook in the first two seconds, the claim to make, what not to say.
  2. Pick creators on a marketplace and ship each a box of product.
  3. Approve, ask for a revision, or reject each video before anyone is paid.
  4. Run the best ones as Meta partnership ads from Kosterina's ad account, shown under the creator's name.
  5. Cut the losers weekly, spend more on the winners, and brief the next round on what worked.Then back to step 1

What AI changes. Finding creators, drafting briefs, tracking who posted and what sold, and flagging which videos to run as ads used to take a team. AI tools now handle most of that, so 300 creators is a one- or two-person job. Meta and TikTok require labels on AI-made content, and for food a real person on camera still carries the trust.

Where Kosterina starts

You already make your own videos on how to tell real olive oil from fake,28 and your best-performing ones are the best brief for creators. Kosterina runs about 42 active Meta ads, all from its own page, and none we could see run under a creator's name.12 You already pay in-store brand ambassadors, mainly at Whole Foods,14 and creators near those same stores are the online version of that team.

Creators already post about Kosterina, from a Hummingbirds campaign sending shoppers to Walmart in November21 to Dr. Nick Norwitz (145K followers) sharing a code for the olive oil shots.22 So the question is how far to scale what's working, not whether to start. Other olive oil brands are already doing it: Graza had food creators posting paid partnerships in November,18 and Cobram Estate hosted a lunch for creators in October.19

Measuring the shelf. Codes and links miss in-store buyers. Run creator ads around one group of stores, hold a similar group back, and compare the sales.

When Alba is worth it

Scale the creators first. For roughly $30,000 to $46,000 in creator pay plus product, you learn which message sells olive oil to which buyer, and you own a library of ads. A celebrity post can't tell you that.

Alba makes sense when all four of these hold:

  • The goal is credibility with retail buyers or national awareness for the shelf, not online sales you can track.
  • The contract lets you run her content as paid ads, for long enough to matter.
  • Her LUNA Bar and Honest commitments don't block your category.
  • There's budget for paid media behind her. LUNA paired her with paid social and shopper ads,3 and one organic post isn't a campaign.

Judge every option on the same five numbers:

  1. Cost per 1,000 people reached
  2. Clicks and code sales
  3. Cost per new customer
  4. Sales in stores near the campaign against similar stores without it
  5. How long you can rerun the content as ads

The store number is the hard one. Tools like Crisp and Daasity pull retailer sales into one dashboard, if you don't already see what sells in which store.

The two aren't exclusive. Creators find the message first, and a star can say it later.

Other brands: Olipop, Shapermint, and the case for a star

Olipop (soda). About 1,900 creators, paid in $36 sample packs plus a commission on sales, no fee per post. Creators drove 12% of total sales in 2024, up from 4% in 2021, and micro-creators' share of those sales rose from 3% to about 20%.7 (These are the vendor's figures.)

Shapermint (shapewear, TikTok Shop). It sells to women 35 to 65, a buyer close to yours, and works with more than 5,000 creators on TikTok Shop, 60% of its top ones over 35. It reports $1.5M in TikTok Shop sales in the first four months of 2026 and is aiming for about $10M for the year (its own figures, and the $10M is a target).29 Worth copying: creators who look like the buyer, filmed at home; its own team instead of an agency; a weekly brief built from the winners;29 and a pay ladder of 10% commission plus free product, rising to 20% after 15 sales and 3 posts in 30 days.30 Its winning hook answers a sizing worry that food doesn't have, and oil is a repeat buy, so judge creators on reorders, not just the first sale.

The case for a star. A DoubleVerify survey of 22,000 consumers (fieldwork February 2026) found 25% said mega-influencers with 1M+ followers had the most impact on their purchases, against 18% for micro.8 DoubleVerify is an ad-verification vendor, and this measures what people say, not sales.
