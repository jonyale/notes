Glossary

DRAM Dynamic Random-Access Memory — the working memory inside servers. AI training and inference need enormous amounts of it, which is why memory pricing became an AI trade.
VWAP Volume-Weighted Average Price — the average price paid per share across a period. Traders use it to tell whether buyers or sellers controlled the session.
TLT The iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF. Its option-implied volatility is the standard read on how much the bond market expects to move.
CPI Consumer Price Index — the monthly US inflation report. "Core" strips out food and energy; "sticky core" tracks only the prices that change slowly.
PJM PJM Interconnection — the grid operator covering much of the eastern US. Its capacity auctions are where future electricity supply gets bought, so a weak auction means a shortfall ahead.
ETF Exchange-Traded Fund. A levered ETF uses borrowed money to multiply the daily move of what it tracks — two or three times, up and down.
Vera Rubin Nvidia's next server platform, following Blackwell. It shifts data-centre power delivery to 800-volt direct current, which is why the names that supply that hardware get re-rated as it rolls out.
Hyperscaler The handful of companies operating data centres at global scale — Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta. They are the ones spending the AI capital budgets, and increasingly borrowing to do it.
Momentum factor A strategy that buys whatever has been going up and sells whatever has been going down. When it unwinds, the best performers get sold hardest for reasons unrelated to their businesses.
Jevons paradox When something gets cheaper to use, total use can rise so much that overall consumption goes up rather than down. The argument for why cheaper AI models could raise chip demand.
Carry trade Borrowing in a cheap currency, usually the yen, to buy higher-yielding assets elsewhere. It unwinds violently when the borrowed currency strengthens.
Breakeven The inflation rate implied by the gap between regular and inflation-linked Treasury yields — the market's own forecast, rather than an economist's.
Credit spread The extra yield a company pays over Treasuries to borrow. Widening spreads mean lenders are charging more for the same risk, which usually shows up before equity trouble.
